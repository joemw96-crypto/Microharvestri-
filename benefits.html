Why microgreens

Small greens, real nutrition, endless uses

Microgreens aren't a garnish — they're one of the most concentrated ways to get vitamins, antioxidants, and flavor into everyday meals. Here's what the research says, and how people actually use them.

The health case for microgreens

Same plants as their mature versions, harvested at the two-leaf stage — when nutrient density peaks.

Up to 40x more nutrients by weight

Studies from the USDA and university researchers have found some microgreens carry significantly higher concentrations of vitamins C, E, and K, plus antioxidants like beta-carotene, than their mature-leaf equivalents.

Sulforaphane from broccoli microgreens

Broccoli microgreens contain the same glucosinolate compounds that convert to sulforaphane in mature broccoli — a compound widely studied for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.

Fiber and plant compounds, low calorie

A serving of microgreens adds meaningful fiber and polyphenols to a meal without adding much in the way of calories — an easy way to boost a plate's nutrition without changing portion sizes.

How people actually use them

Microgreens aren't just a garnish — they hold up as a real ingredient.

1

Finish a dish

A handful on eggs, tacos, avocado toast, or soup adds texture and a fresh, slightly peppery or sweet bite depending on the variety.

2

Build a salad base

Mixed varieties make a salad on their own — no need to wait for lettuce. Radish microgreens add sharpness; pea tendrils add sweetness.

3

Blend into a smoothie

Pea tendril and broccoli microgreens blend smooth and mild — a way to add greens to a smoothie without the bitterness of kale or spinach.

Which variety for which use

Pea Tendril

Sweet, mild, a little grassy

Best for: smoothies, sandwiches, or anyone easing into microgreens for the first time.

Radish

Peppery, sharp, radish-forward

Best for: tacos, eggs, avocado toast — anywhere you want a little kick.

Broccoli

Mild, faintly grassy, slightly sweet

Best for: smoothies, stir-fries, or mixing into salads for a subtle, kid-friendly green.
